Making Tax Digital

Do I needMTD for Income Tax?

Since 6 April 2026, sole traders and landlords whose qualifying income is over £50,000 must keep digital records and send HMRC an update every quarter. Qualifying income is your total self-employment turnover plus gross rental income, before expenses.

From 6 April 2026over £50,000based on your 2024/25 return
From 6 April 2027over £30,000based on your 2025/26 return
From 6 April 2028over £20,000based on your 2026/27 return

Quarterly updates are due on 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May. HMRC doesn’t provide free software, so you’ll need an MTD-compatible product; we can help you choose one.

Already announced

  • April 2027: tax on savings income rises by 2 points, to 22%, 42% and 47%, and property income gets its own rates at the same levels (announced in the Autumn Budget 2025).
  • Frozen to 2031: the Personal Allowance and income tax thresholds stay where they are until April 2031, so more of any pay rise is taxed.
  • Making Tax Digital: the income threshold falls to £30,000 in April 2027 and £20,000 in April 2028.

    Starting a business

    Six things to sortbefore you start

    Talk your plans through with us

    More guides on GOV.UK ↗

    1. 1

      Choose a structure

      Sole trader, partnership or limited company. A company can save tax once profits grow, but brings more paperwork and Companies House filings.

    2. 2

      Plan your capital

      Work out start-up costs and how you’ll fund them: savings, a loan or investors. A simple cash-flow forecast helps when you talk to the bank.

    3. 3

      Check licences and permits

      Food businesses must register with the council at least 28 days before opening. Alcohol, taxis and some trades need a licence.

    4. 4

      Register with HMRC

      Register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the end of the tax year you started trading, or you may be fined. A new company must tell HMRC within 3 months of starting to trade.

    5. 5

      VAT and payroll

      Register for VAT if your turnover goes over £90,000 in any rolling 12 months, and set up PAYE before you pay your first employee.

    6. 6

      Keep digital records from day one

      With Making Tax Digital, sole traders and landlords will need software records anyway. Starting that way saves a painful switch later.
